# Chapter 01: Simple

## Core Idea
To make an idea stick, find its essential core and communicate it through concepts the audience already understands. Relentless prioritization is the key.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Commander's Intent (CI)**: A crisp, plain-talk statement of the ultimate goal, stripped of procedural details. Empowers people to improvise.
- **Inverted Pyramid**: Put the most essential information at the top. Supporting details follow in decreasing importance.
- **Burying the Lead**: The mistake of hiding your core message under mountains of detail.
- **Decision Paralysis**: Too many choices cause anxiety and inaction. Combat with a prioritized core message.
- **Schemas**: Pre-recorded knowledge structures. Anchor new concepts to what people already know (e.g., "pomelo = supersized grapefruit").
- **High-Concept Pitches**: Compact phrases using existing schemas (e.g., "Die Hard on a bus").
- **Generative Analogies**: Metaphors that actively generate new thinking (e.g., Disney's "cast members").

## Key Concepts
- **Core**: The single most important thing, found by stripping away everything else
- **Compact**: Packing profound meaning into limited bandwidth
- **Forced Prioritization**: The painful process of cutting important-but-tangential information

## Key Takeaways
1. "If you say three things, you don't say anything" — find ONE core message
2. Use schemas to make the unfamiliar familiar
3. Generative analogies don't just explain — they shape behavior
4. Commanders Intent works because it empowers improvisation around a shared goal
