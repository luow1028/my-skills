# Chapter 02: Unexpected

## Core Idea
Capture attention by breaking the audience's "guessing machines" with counterintuitive facts, then sustain interest by opening and closing knowledge gaps.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Guessing Machines**: Mental schemas that predict what happens next. Break them to trigger surprise.
- **The Surprise Brow**: The biological response to surprise — eyes widen, body pauses, attention spikes.
- **Post-dictability**: A good surprise makes perfect sense in hindsight. Bad surprises are just gimmicks.
- **Gap Theory of Curiosity**: Curiosity is triggered by a gap in knowledge. Point out what people DON'T know before giving them facts.

## Key Concepts
- **Common sense is the enemy**: "Uncommon sense" breaks patterns and grabs attention
- **Knowledge gaps**: Open the gap first (pose the mystery), then close it (deliver the answer)
- **Post-dictable**: The surprise should feel inevitable once you understand the core truth

## Key Takeaways
1. Don't start with facts — start with what's missing from the audience's knowledge
2. Surprise without insight is a gimmick; surprise WITH insight is sticky
3. Force people to commit to predictions to expose their overconfidence
4. The news-teaser approach works because it exploits the Gap Theory
