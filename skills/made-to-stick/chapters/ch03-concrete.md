# Chapter 03: Concrete

## Core Idea
Abstract ideas are hard to understand and remember. Ground everything in vivid, sensory, human-scale physical realities.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Velcro Theory of Memory**: The more sensory "hooks" you attach to an idea, the better it clings to memory. Abstract ideas have no hooks.
- **Computing in Context**: Explain abstract concepts by anchoring them to concrete, real-world situations.

## Key Concepts
- **Concrete**: Language that can be perceived by the senses — what you can see, hear, touch
- **Sensory hooks**: Physical details that make ideas grab onto memory
- **Shared turf**: A concrete context where experts and novices can collaborate

## Key Takeaways
1. Statistics are meaningful only when they illustrate a relationship — not as raw data
2. Translate abstract goals into concrete actions ("landscapes" not "maximize shareholder value")
3. Concrete language is the universal language — it works for novices and experts alike
4. Use physical simulations and shared spaces to bridge the expert-novice gap
