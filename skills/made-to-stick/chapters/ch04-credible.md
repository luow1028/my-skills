# Chapter 04: Credible

## Core Idea
Make people believe your ideas through internal credibility — vivid details, relatable statistics, or challenges that let the audience verify claims themselves.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Anti-authorities**: Real people who lived the negative consequences (e.g., a smoker with lung cancer) instead of formal experts
- **Human-Scale Principle**: Statistics become meaningful when scaled to human experience
- **Sinatra Test**: "If I can make it there, I'll make it anywhere" — one extreme success story can establish credibility for all contexts
- **Testable Credentials**: Let the audience verify claims themselves rather than trusting authority

## Key Concepts
- **Internal credibility**: Details, statistics, and challenges embedded in the message itself
- **External credibility**: Celebrity endorsements and expert authority (weaker than internal)
- **Statistics for relationships**: Use stats to show a relationship, not as standalone proof

## Key Takeaways
1. A single anti-authority can be more persuasive than 100 experts
2. Make statistics human-scale ("a football field of forest" not "10 million acres")
3. The Sinatra Test: one compelling example can establish universal credibility
4. Testable credentials beat appeals to authority
