# Cheatsheet — Made to Stick

**QUICK REFERENCE: MADE TO STICK**

### **RULES OF THUMB**
*   **The Curse of Knowledge:** Once you know something, it is nearly impossible to imagine what it is like not to know it. You must actively translate your "tune" into concepts novices can grasp [1, 2].
*   **The Rule of Three:** "If you say three things, you don't say anything" [3]. Forced prioritization is required to find the core message [4].
*   **The Velcro Theory of Memory:** The more sensory, concrete "hooks" you put into an idea, the better it will cling to memory [5-7].
*   **The Mother Teresa Principle:** "If I look at the mass, I will never act. If I look at the one, I will." People are wired to feel empathy for a single individual, not an abstract group or a statistic [8, 9].
*   **Statistics are for Relationships:** Statistics are rarely meaningful in and of themselves; they should be used to illustrate an underlying relationship, not as raw data [10, 11].
*   **Fight Sticky with Stickier:** You cannot "unstick" a false rumor or urban legend by denying it with facts. You must shift the turf by propagating a new, competing idea that is even more sticky [12-14].
*   **Stories over Morals:** You can reconstruct a moral from a story, but you cannot reconstruct a story from a moral [15].

### **IF-X-THEN-Y RULES**

**Attention & Interest**
*   **IF** your audience is tuning you out, **THEN** break their "guessing machines" by presenting a counter-intuitive fact or "uncommon sense" to trigger surprise [16-18].
*   **IF** you need to sustain interest over a long period, **THEN** use the "Gap Theory of Curiosity." Point out a specific gap in their knowledge or pose a mystery *before* you give them the facts [19, 20].
*   **IF** your audience is overconfident and doesn't realize they have a knowledge gap, **THEN** force them to publicly commit to a prediction to expose their flawed schema [21, 22].

**Understanding & Coordination**
*   **IF** your team is suffering from "decision paralysis" due to too much complexity, **THEN** provide a "Commander's Intent"—a crisp, stripped-down statement of the ultimate goal to guide their behavior [23-25].
*   **IF** you need to teach a complex or brand-new concept quickly, **THEN** use a "Generative Analogy" or schema (e.g., explaining a pomelo as a "supersized grapefruit") to anchor the new idea to existing knowledge [26-28].
*   **IF** experts and novices (or different departments) are talking past each other, **THEN** force the communication down to a concrete, physical level (a shared turf) where everyone can bring their knowledge to bear [29-31].

**Belief & Action**
*   **IF** you lack external authority (like a celebrity or expert) to back up your claim, **THEN** use an "anti-authority" (someone who has lived the negative consequences) [32, 33] or issue a "testable credential" that allows the audience to verify the claim themselves [34, 35].
*   **IF** your audience has their "Analytical Hat" on and is evaluating your statistics coldly, **THEN** drop the numbers and tell a story about a specific person to trigger empathy and action [36, 37].
*   **IF** a pure self-interest appeal (greed/money) insults your audience or falls flat, **THEN** get out of "Maslow's Basement" and appeal to their group identity (e.g., "What does a person like me do in this situation?") [38-40].

### **DECISION TABLES**

#### **TABLE 1: Diagnosing Communication Failures**
Use this table to fix messages that aren't working [41-47].

| **Symptom** | **Root Cause** | **SUCCESs Solution** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "No one is listening." | Predictable, common-sense pattern. | **Unexpected:** Surprise them. Break their guessing machines with counter-intuitive insight. |
| "I lose them halfway through." | Lack of narrative momentum. | **Unexpected:** Create a curiosity gap or a mystery that is slowly solved over time. |
| "They nod, but don't take action." | Message is too abstract or complex. | **Simple & Concrete:** Use generative analogies, human-scale examples, and strip away jargon. |
| "They aren't buying it / They argue." | Lack of internal credibility. | **Credible:** Use telling details, the Sinatra Test, or a springboard story to shift them into creative mode. |
| "They are apathetic / Unfired up." | Analytical thinking or semantic stretch. | **Emotional:** Focus on individuals (Mother Teresa effect), or appeal to higher identity/ideals. |
| "Nothing happens after the meeting." | Lack of a mental flight simulator. | **Stories:** Inspire them with a Challenge Plot, or give them a clear "Commander's Intent" proverb. |

#### **TABLE 2: Selecting the Right Credibility Tool**
When you need to make people believe but lack traditional authority [10, 32, 34, 48-50].

| **If your situation is...** | **Use this Credibility Tool...** | **Example** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| You need raw, experiential proof | **Anti-Authority** | A dying smoker with emphysema advocating against tobacco. |
| You have an extreme past success | **The Sinatra Test** | "If we delivered Harry Potter books safely in India, we can deliver your movies." |
| You need to prove a relationship | **Human-Scale Statistics** | Equating nuclear warheads to the deafening sound of 5,000 BBs hitting a metal bucket. |
| You want immediate, irrefutable trust | **Testable Credentials** | Wendy's asking customers to look at competitors' burgers and ask, "Where's the beef?" |
| You need your idea to feel "real" | **Vivid Details** | Mentioning a child's "Darth Vader toothbrush" to prove a mother's attentiveness. |

#### **TABLE 3: Selecting the Right Story Plot**
Stories provide simulation (how to act) and inspiration (energy to act). Choose the right plot for your specific motivational goal [51-55].

| **Your Goal** | **Plot Type** | **How It Works** | **Example** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inspire Perseverance & Grit** | **Challenge Plot** | A protagonist overcomes daunting, formidable obstacles to succeed. | Jared Fogle losing 245 pounds by eating fast food; David and Goliath. |
| **Inspire Tolerance & Teamwork** | **Connection Plot** | People develop a relationship that bridges a deep social, racial, or demographic gap. | The Good Samaritan; the Mean Joe Greene Coke commercial. |
| **Inspire Innovation & Initiative** | **Creativity Plot** | Someone solves a long-standing puzzle or problem with novel resourcefulness. | Tying plastic to a rental car bumper for a quick "Drag Test"; MacGyver. |
