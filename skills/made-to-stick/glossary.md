# Glossary — Made to Stick

Here is a comprehensive glossary of the key terms, frameworks, and conceptual vocabulary from the book, arranged in alphabetical order:

**Abstraction** — A high-level, expert way of thinking that lacks sensory details and often hinders a novice's ability to understand new concepts [1, 2].

**Anti-authority** — A source of credibility that relies on real people who have lived the negative consequences of a situation, rather than formal experts or celebrities [3, 4].

**Burying the lead** — A communication mistake where the most essential element of a message is hidden or placed too far down in the structure [5, 6].

**Challenge Plot** — A story narrative where a protagonist overcomes formidable odds and daunting obstacles to succeed, inspiring perseverance and grit [7-9].

**CHIFF** — An acronym standing for "Clever, High-quality, Innovative, Friendly, Fun" that Cranium uses as a compact, actionable statement of strategic differentiation [10, 11].

**Commander's Intent (CI)** — A crisp, plain-talk statement specifying the ultimate goal of a plan, which empowers individuals to improvise and adapt in unpredictable environments [12-14].

**Compact** — The trait of packing profound meaning into a succinct message that fits within the limited bandwidth of human cognition [15-17].

**Computing in Context** — An educational method that explains abstract mathematical concepts by anchoring them to concrete, familiar, and real-world situations [18, 19].

**Concrete** — Language or ideas that can be perceived by the human senses, serving as a universal language that helps novices understand and remember [2, 20, 21].

**Connection Plot** — A story narrative focusing on people who develop a relationship that bridges a significant social, racial, or demographic gap, inspiring tolerance and love [9, 22, 23].

**Core** — The absolute essential essence of a message, discovered by relentlessly stripping away superfluous and tangential elements [24, 25].

**Creativity Plot** — A story narrative involving someone who makes a mental breakthrough or solves a long-standing puzzle with innovative resourcefulness [9, 26, 27].

**Curse of Knowledge** — A psychological cognitive bias where an individual possessing specific information finds it nearly impossible to imagine what it is like not to know it [28, 29].

**Decision Paralysis** — The anxiety and inability to act that occurs when people are faced with too much complexity, uncertainty, or an abundance of good options [30-32].

**Fight sticky with stickier** — The strategy of combating a false rumor or unhelpful idea by replacing it with a competing idea that is even more memorable and compelling [33, 34].

**Forced Prioritization** — The painful but necessary process of weeding out important but tangential information to let the single most critical core message shine [5, 35].

**Gap Theory of Curiosity** — The behavioral theory that curiosity is triggered when we realize there is a gap in our knowledge, creating a mental "itch" that we need to scratch [36-38].

**Generative Analogy** — A metaphor that substitutes something easy to think about for something difficult, actively generating new perceptions and guiding future behavior [39-41].

**Guessing Machines** — The mental schemas and expectations we use to predict what will happen next, which must be intentionally broken to generate surprise [42, 43].

**High-Concept Pitch** — A compact, proverbial phrase that packs immense meaning into a new idea by relying on existing schemas, frequently used in Hollywood to accelerate understanding [44, 45].

**Human-Scale Principle** — The practice of translating abstract or massive statistics into everyday, tangible contexts so people can bring their intuition to bear [46-48].

**Identity Model of Decision-Making** — A psychological model where people make choices based on their self-image by asking what someone like them would do in a specific situation [49-51].

**Internal Credibility** — The believability an idea carries on its own merits, established through vivid details, relatable statistics, or testable credentials rather than external authority [52, 53].

**Inverted Pyramid** — A journalistic structure where the most essential and important information is placed at the very top of a communication [17, 54].

**Maslow's Basement** — The false psychological assumption that while we are motivated by higher-level needs like self-actualization, other people are primarily motivated by base needs like money and physical security [55].

**Mental Simulation** — The process of mentally rehearsing an action or sequence of events, which evokes the same brain modules as physical activity and actively prepares people to act [56-58].

**Mother Teresa Principle** — The behavioral truth that people are wired to feel empathy and take action for a single, identifiable individual rather than for abstract causes or massive statistical groups [59-61].

**Post-dictability** — A characteristic of a satisfying surprise where an unexpected twist makes perfect sense in hindsight once the core message is revealed [43, 62].

**Proverb** — A short sentence drawn from long experience that serves as the ultimate model of simplicity by being both compact and profound [63-65].

**Schemas** — Mental structures or collections of generic properties stored in our memories about a specific concept or category [66, 67].

**Semantic Stretch** — The natural linguistic process where people overuse a term that delivers an emotional kick, thereby diluting its underlying power and meaning over time [68-70].

**Sinatra Test** — A credibility-building concept where succeeding in one extraordinarily difficult or high-profile example alone is enough to establish authority in a given domain [71, 72].

**Springboard Story** — A narrative that communicates possibilities by showing how an existing problem might change, bypassing skepticism and shifting the audience into an active problem-solving mode [73, 74].

**Sticky Idea** — A concept that is understandable, memorable, and effective in changing thought or behavior [75-77].

**SUCCESs Framework** — A six-principle checklist identifying the traits that make ideas sticky: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, and Stories [76, 78, 79].

**Surprise Brow** — The distinctive facial expression of surprise that widens the eyes and expands the field of vision, forcing the body to pause and take in new information [80, 81].

**Tappers and Listeners** — An experiment and metaphor demonstrating the Curse of Knowledge, where people tapping a song's rhythm falsely predict that uninformed listeners will easily guess the tune [82-84].

**Testable Credential** — A claim that explicitly challenges the audience to verify the truth of a message for themselves, serving as a "try before you buy" approach to credibility [72, 85, 86].

**The "Three Whys"** — A tactic of repeatedly asking "Why?" to bypass the Curse of Knowledge and connect abstract actions to deeper, emotional core values [87, 88].

**Velcro Theory of Memory** — The concept that the human brain is like the loop side of Velcro, and concrete, sensory details act as the hooks that snag ideas into long-term memory [2, 89, 90].

**WIIFY (What's In It For You)** — A direct appeal to the audience's self-interest that spells out the exact personal benefits they will gain from an idea [51, 91].
